# THE LONG SETTLEMENT — story bible

Working title of the game: **The Long Settlement**. An original world. Do not
borrow names, creatures, terms or plot beats from any existing game.

Tone: a mercenary-academy coming-of-age and a pilgrimage toward a sacrifice,
told with the paranoia of a spy thriller. Sincere, never ironic. Adults talk
like adults: about debt, law, leverage and who is allowed to use force. The
love story is slow, mostly unsaid, and built from small acts of attention.

## The world: Aurelle

An archipelago of island-cities on a sea that is not always there.

**The Undertow.** Once a generation the sea withdraws from an island and does
not come back as water. What returns is stillness: a tide that erases. People
on a taken island are not killed. They are *forgotten*: names leave records,
faces leave memory. The official word is that the island was **foreclosed**.

**The Long Settlement.** Four hundred years ago the founders made a covenant
with the Undertow: the islands owe it a debt, and the debt can be serviced.
Each generation one person, the **Bearer**, walks the pilgrimage through the
shrine of every city (an **Orrery**) and at the end is *spent*: the Bearer's
whole remembered life is paid over, and the Undertow sleeps another thirty
years. Everyone believes this. It is mostly true, which is what makes the lie
in it hard to see.

**Calm.** The thing the sacrifice buys is called calm. The currency, the
**mark**, is legally a claim on calm: every note reads "payable in stillness".
The **Mint of Aurelle** issues marks against its reserve of **stillsalt**, a
mineral mined in a few towns that holds calm the way a battery holds charge.

**Writs.** In Aurelle force is licensed. To carry a blade, sail a warship,
break a strike or defend a pilgrim you need a **Writ**, issued by the Mint and
allotted, by ancient law, *by lot*: the **Reels of Assay**, a three-reel
drawing machine in every Mint hall. The founders made it a lottery so that no
faction could buy a monopoly on violence. Whoever controls the reels controls
who may act. (In the game, the slot machine is the Reels of Assay, writs are
credits, and the steel pinball is the **Plumb**, the sphere a Bearer's party
sends through each city's Orrery.)

**The Drawing.** The Bearer is also chosen by the Reels, from a drum holding
the name of every citizen of eighteen years. By law.

## The powers (the balance of power is the plot)

- **The Mint** (government, in practice). Issues marks and writs. Run by
  **Chancellor Benedikt Corvane**. Its secret police are the **Audit**.
- **Halcyon Surety** (corporation). Began as an insurer of pilgrimages and
  cargo; now trains its own licensed soldiers at **Halcyon Academy** and sells
  protection by contract. A Halcyon guardian is called a **Surety**: a person
  who personally guarantees someone else's obligation. Halcyon holds more
  municipal debt than anyone knows.
- **The Compact** (labour). The stevedores' and miners' union. No writs, so no
  legal force; its only weapons are the strike and the mutual-aid fund, the
  **Commons**.
- **The Benches** (law). Magistrates. Poor, proud, slow, and the one power
  everyone still pretends to obey.

The secret, revealed slowly across the whole game (only seeded in chapters 1-2):
the debt to the Undertow is real, but a Bearer's death does not pay it down. It
*refinances* it. The founders knew. Each Settlement rolls the principal forward
thirty years with interest, and the interest is paid in forgotten islands. The
Mint and Halcyon both know and both profit: the Mint because scarce calm is
what gives the mark value, Halcyon because it sells **Settlement bonds** that
pay out when a Bearer is spent, and it has learned that the Reels can be made
to land where it likes.

## Economics the player should feel, not be lectured on

- **Debt and garnishment.** When the player is broke the Mint advances marks
  at interest and garnishes future earnings. Characters notice arrears.
- **The Commons.** Trust earned in a town through *commons* missions lets the
  town cover a lost ball for nothing. The Mint lends; the Commons gives. The
  story argues, through play, that trust is also a currency and a harder one.
- **Rigged chance.** The first spin of chapter 1 is rigged by the game itself.
  The player will not know until the epilogue says so.

Espionage layer: Thorne runs Ren as an asset for Halcyon without telling him
that is what he is. His "exam tasks" are tradecraft: plant a listener, lift a
seal, photograph a ledger. Sable runs the Audit's counter-operation. Corvane
knows about both and lets them run. Ilse is the one honest actor, which is why
all three need her.

## Chapter 1 — THE DRAWING

Table `vessery`. Night of the Festival of Lots in the harbour city of Vessery.

Element ids on this table: bumpers `drum1` `drum2` `drum3`; left target bank
`lanternA` `lanternB` `lanternC`; right target bank `sealM` `sealI` `sealN`
`sealT`; top lanes `lotL` `lotC` `lotR`; left ramp `pierRamp`; right orbit
`dockOrbit`; saucer `bench`; plus `inL` `inR` `outL` `outR` `slingL` `slingR`.

**Prologue.** The Ledger introduces Aurelle, the debt, the Drawing. Ren on a
rooftop above the festival, Thorne in his ear: tonight is his field exam; pass
and his scholarship debt is forgiven and he graduates a Surety. Then the game
hands the player the Reels. **Rigged spin: three `orb`**, "The Bearer's Lot",
paying 3 writs. Nobody remarks on it yet.

Scenes:

1. `ch1.s1` **Lantern Watch** (commons). all `lanternA-C`. Nan Okerby's stall
   was knocked over by Mint stewards clearing the route. Ren, against orders,
   helps relight her lanterns. She tells him every lantern carries the name of
   someone the Undertow took, written by someone who no longer remembers why
   the name matters. Ren has carried a blank one every year since he was six.
2. `ch1.s2` **The Listener** (covert). saucer `bench` once. Thorne's first
   real task: place a listening shell in Magistrate Maravel's chamber, "to
   verify the certification, routine". Ren does it and is caught at once by
   Ilse, who assumes he is a lost steward and makes him carry files. First
   meeting: she is whistling; she is arguing with her father that the drum
   audit was waived this year, which is unlawful; the Magistrate says the
   waiver is itself lawful, which is worse.
3. `ch1.s3` **Weighted Drums** (covert/duty). count 12 on `drum1-3`. Tobb,
   taking bets on which quarter the Bearer will come from, shows Ren his
   table: the dock quarters are over-represented in the drum four to one. The
   dockers know. The Compact gathers. Ren is ordered to hold the line with a
   writ he drew an hour ago.
4. `ch1.s4` **The Drawing** (duty). all `lotL` `lotC` `lotR`. The great Reels
   turn. Three orbs. The name is not from the dock quarter at all: **Ilse
   Maravel**. Her father, as certifying magistrate, must countersign his own
   daughter's draw, and does, because if a magistrate may refuse, so may
   anyone. She thanks him for it. This is the emotional centre of the chapter.
5. `ch1.s5` **The Pier** (duty). count 3 on `pierRamp`. A shot from the crowd,
   meant for Ilse. Ren gets her out along the pier. The shooter wears a
   docker's coat and Audit boots; Ren sees the boots and tells no one. Ilse,
   shaking, asks him to tell her something true. He tells her about the
   blank lantern.
6. `ch1.s6` **Seal of the Mint** (duty). all `sealM` `sealI` `sealN` `sealT`.
   Corvane's office. Halcyon has won the pilgrimage contract; Ren, who was
   merely nearest, is named her Surety, bound body-for-body. Corvane explains
   a surety bond with great courtesy. Ilse reads the contract aloud and finds
   it has a schedule she is not permitted to see. Ren signs.
7. `ch1.s7` **Cast Off** (commons). sequence `dockOrbit`, `pierRamp`, `bench`.
   Before dawn the dockers, who came to riot, instead line the pier with
   lanterns for her. Nan gives Ren a lantern with a name on it at last: his
   own. Father and daughter say goodbye badly, as people do.

**Epilogue.** Ferry at dawn. Then, over the Line, Thorne reporting to someone
unheard: "The reels landed as arranged. The asset is in place. No, he does
not know. That is what makes him good." Cut to black.

## Chapter 2 — THE PRICE OF CALM

Table `saltmere`. The first shrine town: a stillsalt mining town in arrears.

Element ids on this table: bumpers `shaft1` `shaft2` `shaft3`; left target
bank `strikeA` `strikeB` `strikeC`; right target bank `lienL` `lienI` `lienE`
`lienN`; top lanes `saltL` `saltC` `saltR`; left ramp `causewayRamp`; right
orbit `courtOrbit`; saucer `orrery`; plus `inL` `inR` `outL` `outR` `slingL`
`slingR`.

**Prologue.** The causeway. Ilse has read the public part of her contract
forty times. Tobb, who has attached himself to the party "to protect a
position", explains Settlement bonds: paper that pays when a Bearer is spent.
Somebody bought a great deal of it the week before the Drawing. No rigged
spin (`riggedSpin: null`).

Scenes:

1. `ch2.s1` **The Toll** (duty). count 2 on `causewayRamp`. Saltmere cannot
   pay its calm-levy; the Mint has posted a notice of foreclosure, which here
   is not a metaphor. The Orrery is locked behind a toll the town itself
   cannot afford. Hesper Dunmore refuses the Bearer entry: "Every pilgrim who
   comes through here leaves us poorer and tells us it is holy."
2. `ch2.s2` **Bread on the Line** (commons). all `strikeA-C`. Ren and Ilse do
   not force the gate. They work a shift in the Commons kitchen. Ilse writes
   letters for miners who cannot read their own eviction notices. Hesper
   watches Ren wash pots for three hours and decides something about him.
3. `ch2.s3` **Down the Shafts** (covert). count 15 on `shaft1-3`. Thorne wants
   the mine's assay book photographed. Hesper takes them down anyway. The
   galleries are nearly bare: the stillsalt is running out, here and, Tobb
   guesses, everywhere. The mark is backed by a reserve that is not there.
   The Mint is foreclosing towns to hide a shortfall.
4. `ch2.s4` **The Hearing** (duty). count 3 on `courtOrbit`. Ilse petitions the
   circuit bench for a stay of foreclosure, arguing that a debt owed in calm
   cannot be collected by destroying the debtor's means to produce it. She is
   magnificent and eighteen. The Assessor for the Mint is **Sable Vasker**.
   The brothers meet across a courtroom. The stay is granted for thirty days.
5. `ch2.s5` **Whose Paper** (covert). all `lienL` `lienI` `lienE` `lienN`. Tobb
   and Ren break into the records vault to learn who holds Saltmere's debt.
   Not the Mint. It was sold on months ago, to Halcyon Surety. Ren's own
   employer profits if the town is taken and profits again when Ilse is spent.
   Ren does not report this to Thorne. First lie to his handler.
6. `ch2.s6` **The Orrery** (duty). saucer `orrery` once. The miners open the
   shrine themselves. The rite: the Plumb is set turning and the Bearer pays
   the shrine's price to carry its calm onward. The price is a memory, taken
   whole. Ilse comes out smiling and steady. On the walk back Ren whistles her
   lullaby without thinking. She asks, pleasantly, what the tune is.
7. `ch2.s7` **What the Tide Is Owed** (commons). all `saltL` `saltC` `saltR`.
   Ren asks Hesper and the Commons to keep an account: a book of what Ilse
   loses at each shrine, held in trust by people who will remember it for
   her. The town that would not let her in becomes the first keeper of her
   ledger. Ren writes the first entry: her mother's song, note by note.

**Epilogue.** Night shore. Sable, alone with Ren, off the record: the Audit
knows the Reels were touched and believes Ren touched them. Then Thorne on the
Line with new orders, reading the sealed schedule to Ren's contract at last: a
Surety guarantees the obligation, not the person. If the Bearer strays from
the pilgrimage, the Surety is to *settle the account himself*. Ren looks at
Ilse asleep by the fire, whistling nothing. Cut to black. "End of chapter two."

## Writing rules

- Every blocking cinematic line should be speakable in one breath: 5 to 30
  words. Cutscenes run 14 to 30 lines; prologues and epilogues 12 to 24.
- No stage directions in `text`. Staging goes in `bg`, `on`, `cam`, `emote`.
- Economics and law arrive as stakes inside a scene, said by someone who wants
  something. Never a lecture.
- Each cutscene must change a relationship, not only deliver plot.
- Briefings and barks are short (under 12 words) because the ball is moving.
