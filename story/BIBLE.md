# THE LONG SETTLEMENT — story bible

An original world. Do not borrow names, creatures, terms or plot beats from
any existing game. In particular this is NOT a pilgrimage story: there is no
chosen sacrifice, no shrine circuit, no recurring monster, no church hiding a
cycle, no summoning. The stakes are political and personal: a treaty, a vote,
a currency, and who gets cut off from the world when the money runs short.

Tone: a mercenary-academy coming-of-age inside a spy thriller, with the
melancholy of a long sea journey. Sincere, never ironic. Adults talk like
adults: about debt, law, leverage and who is allowed to use force. The love
story is slow, mostly unsaid, and built from small acts of attention.

## The world: Aurelle

An archipelago of forty island-cities on a violent sea.

**The Churn.** The sea between the islands is not navigable on its own:
standing storms, shifting bars, fog that lasts a season. Nothing mystical is
claimed for it. It is weather, and it has always been there.

**Stillsalt and the lights.** A mineral, stillsalt, burns with a heavy cold
flame whose smoke lays the sea flat for miles. Every island keeps a great
brass lamp-engine, its **orrery**, burning stillsalt day and night. Where two
islands' lights overlap there is a **lane**, and on a lane there is trade,
post, medicine and news. Where a light goes out the lane closes within a week.
The island is not destroyed. It is simply no longer reachable. The legal word
is **foreclosed**; people say an island has **gone dark**. Nobody knows what
becomes of the people on a dark island. Eleven islands are dark.

**The mark.** The currency is legally a claim on stillsalt: every note reads
"payable in calm". The **Mint of Aurelle** issues marks against its stillsalt
reserve and supplies each island's light on credit, against a calm-levy. An
island in arrears has its salt ration stopped. So the Mint decides who stays
on the map.

**The Long Settlement.** Twenty-eight years ago the islands ended the
Lanewar, a generation of cities putting out one another's lights, with a
treaty. Its terms: no city keeps an army. Force is licensed, per act, by a
**Writ**, and writs are allotted *by lot*, on the **Reels of Assay**, a
three-reel drawing machine in every Mint hall, so that no faction can buy a
monopoly on violence. Whoever controls the reels controls who may act. (In
the game the slot machine is the Reels of Assay, writs are credits, and the
steel pinball is the **Plumb**: the plumb-weight emblem of a Surety, "the
weight on the line".)

**The Tribune and the Casting Vote.** The Settlement runs for seven-year
terms and must be renewed by the Assembly of Cities, which has deadlocked at
every renewal since the first. The founders foresaw that and distrusted
every office they could imagine, so the tie is broken by an ordinary
citizen chosen by the Reels from a drum holding every adult name in the
host city: the **Tribune of the Lot**. The Tribune must hold a public hearing
in each of seven cities, a journey called **the Circuit**, then travel to the
capital and cast the single **Casting Vote**: renew the Settlement, or let it
lapse. For the term the Tribune by law "has no kin and no trade": all family
and professional ties are suspended, so that no one may claim them. Of the
last three Tribunes, one was drowned on the Circuit, one vanished, and one
came home rich and is not spoken to.

This year is a renewal year. The Drawing is in Vessery.

## The powers (the balance of power is the plot)

- **The Mint** (government, in practice). Issues marks and writs, supplies the
  lights. Run by **Chancellor Benedikt Corvane**. Its investigators are the
  **Audit**. The Mint wants the Settlement renewed with new emergency powers.
- **Halcyon Surety** (corporation). Began as an insurer of cargo and lanes;
  now trains licensed soldiers at **Halcyon Academy** and sells protection by
  contract. A Halcyon guardian is a **Surety**: a person who personally
  guarantees someone else's obligation, body for body. Halcyon has quietly
  bought up island debt, and holds a great deal of paper that pays if the
  Settlement *lapses*: without the treaty, every city may hire an army, and
  Halcyon is the only one for sale.
- **The Compact** (labour). The stevedores' and miners' union. No writs, so no
  lawful force; its weapons are the strike and its mutual-aid fund, the
  **Commons**. Wants the treaty, hates the Mint.
- **The Benches** (law). Magistrates. Poor, proud, slow, and the one power
  everyone still pretends to obey.

The secret, revealed slowly across the whole game (only seeded in chapters
1-2): the stillsalt is running out. The mark is backed by a reserve that is
no longer there. The Mint has been foreclosing islands not to punish arrears
but to ration salt, choosing who goes dark and calling it debt. Halcyon knows,
and has rigged this year's Drawing so the Casting Vote is carried by someone
it believes it can steer or stop.

## Economics the player should feel, not be lectured on

- **Debt and garnishment.** When the player is broke the Mint advances marks
  at interest and garnishes future earnings. Characters notice arrears.
- **The Commons.** Trust earned in a town through *commons* missions lets the
  town cover a lost ball for nothing. The Mint lends; the Commons gives. The
  story argues, through play, that trust is also a currency and a harder one.
- **Rigged chance.** The first spin of chapter 1 is rigged by the game itself.
  The player will not know until the epilogue says so.

   he was six, has never been owed anything and does not know what to do with

   toward the dark islands every spring, an old custom, and that in the
   direction of Carrow, on clear nights, some of them swear there is a light.

**Epilogue.** Night shore. Sable, alone with Ren, off the record: the Audit
knows the Reels were touched and believes Ren touched them. Then Thorne on the
Line with new orders, reading the sealed schedule to Ren's contract at last:
a Surety guarantees the obligation, not the person, and Halcyon has
guaranteed how the Casting Vote will fall. If the Tribune means to renew, she
is not to reach the capital: a vote not cast is a lapse. Ren looks at Ilse
asleep by the fire. Cut to black. "End of chapter two."

## The ending: one delusion, two outcomes

Every power in Aurelle acts rationally on its incentives, and the back half
of the game unwinds them one by one: Halcyon's position collapses when its
own people testify and its bond is exposed; the Audit's loyalty fails when
Sable learns what the Mint is rationing; the Compact's secrecy ends when the
dark islands speak. By the capital, nobody's incentive holds the system up
any more. What is left is one man's belief.

**Corvane's delusion.** Benedikt Corvane is not greedy and not cruel. He
holds exactly one false belief, and holds it as a kindness: *people cannot be
trusted with the truth; told the sum, they will always break.* Everything he
has done follows correctly from that. It is why he rations in secret, why he
calls it debt, why he lets two spy services run rather than tell either the
whole of it, and why he has never once asked the islands what they would do.
He was a young clerk in the Lanewar and watched a city put out its
neighbour's light over a rumour. He has been right ever since, in his own
accounts, because he has never run the test.

The belief is what undoes him, in both endings. The answer to the shortage
exists and has existed for nine years: the dark islands learned to stretch
salt with kelp-ash, three parts to one, enough for all forty lights at a
dimmer flame. It was found by the people he wrote off, and it only works if
every island shares its reserve openly. He cannot believe they would.

**It can end either way, and the player has been deciding all along.** The
game keeps two running totals across every chapter: trust earned from
commons missions, and loans taken from the Mint. At the capital, when the
truth is read into the record and Corvane, certain of a riot, puts out the
harbour light to force emergency powers:

- **The Lane Held** (trust outweighs debt). Nobody riots. The city rows out
  with lanterns and holds the lane by hand until the dark islands' signal
  lamps answer and the dimmer flame is lit across the map. Corvane's own
  stewards set down their writs. He is undone by being wrong in public, and
  the last thing he does is ask, honestly, how they knew it would hold.
  Nobody knew. That is the answer. Ilse casts the vote to renew on new terms.
- **The Sum** (debt outweighs trust). The crowd breaks, as he said it would.
  He gets his emergency powers and is proved right, and is undone anyway: a
  man who trusts no one has no one, and Halcyon, which he believed he alone
  could manage, forecloses on the Mint itself within the week. The treaty
  lapses. Ren and Ilse take the under-lane to Carrow, where one small light
  is kept by people who share it. The world is smaller and still there.

Neither ending is a punishment. The second is the world the player's own
ledger describes. Chapters 3 to 5 must seed this without naming it: Corvane
is always reasonable, always sure how a crowd will behave, and every commons
scene quietly shows a crowd behaving otherwise. Kelp-ash is mentioned once,
in chapter 4, as a poor island's make-do, and nobody remarks on it.

horizon: a light on the lane ahead, the Orm light, goes out a week before
its notice. "End of chapter three."

## Established facts (chapters 1 to 5; later chapters must respect these)

**Ren.** 19. Carrow went dark thirteen years ago, when he was six; every
"years owed", "years dark" or "years at the Reach" figure for him is
thirteen. Nineteen is only his age (and his old bunk, bed nineteen; and
Tessaly's nineteen years dark). Born **Renny Lowrie**: the name was on his
clinic tag, and a dormitory clerk issued him "Vasker". He has said he will
break the bond as Vasker and then wishes "to be asked again" about Lowrie;
Ilse has not yet asked. His mother alone said yes to the clinic boat and told
him "nine days"; Thorne, who collected him, repeated "nine days" knowing the
foreclosure date. He had sisters, the eldest nine. Carrow was dark four
years and has shown a small lamp for nine; a woman keeps it and signs
"Lowrie". Which woman is not known. Her one message: "Renny. Is he fed."
His reply: yes, he is coming by the long way round, he floated a lantern.

**Ren's paper.** Halcyon bought Carrow's debt a month before the order,
chartered the clinic boat for twelve minors and recovered one. His file
reads: collateral, appreciating, thirteen entries. Clause twenty of the
indenture ("a true account, open to the Scholar on request") is how he got
it. The bursar's write-off of his debt is void on default. Contract number
**417**; the sealed order is Schedule One. At Tallis he declined on the
record of the Circuit; the bond is void, his writs and licence are gone, the
thirteen years fall due at once. In law he is a volunteer.

**The vote bond.** Three pages: Halcyon guarantees the Settlement lapses,
sum assured greater than the Reach is worth, payable to nine numbered
accounts, witnessed O. Thorne. Lapse bonds pay twelve for one and were
bought through nine brokers; the same nine sold the mark short at Tallis.

**Ilse and Ren.** She learned of the listening shell at Halcyon Reach (from
Sable, with the signals log naming Ren and his handler) and called him
"Surety", six paces behind, from then until the net was finished on Little
Brand, where he told her everything unasked, the sealed order included. She
has called him "Ren" since. She has said she means to renew, on new terms.
The shell is a separate account and is not settled. On the Tallis steps she
told him to stand beside her, not behind. He first calls her "Ilse" there.

**The lullaby.** "Low the lamp and long the lane. / Hush, the water's lying
still. / Who goes out comes home again. / Keep the light upon the sill." It
was a rowing chant before it was a lullaby; the under-lane rowers pull to
it. Ilse stopped whistling at the Saltmere hearing, whistled the whole tune
leaving Little Brand, and again on the Tallis steps. Her father taps the
next line on a table edge.

**Tobb.** Mint actuary nine years, struck off for the lung table (what a
miner's lungs are worth). Informed on the party to Corvane from the night
of the Drawing; quit after the Saltmere mine; Corvane replied "You will find
that you are not." He also sold the bearing of the under-lane. He confessed
in the boat before he was caught. He is used, for his arithmetic, and is
not forgiven.

**Orm and the dark islands.** Eleven islands are dark and all eleven are
alive, signalling by shuttered kelp-ash lamps; the Compact supplies them by
the under-lane and has kept the secret. Orm is one of the eleven: its
people were foreclosed years ago, but the Mint kept the Orm light burning
for the Halcyon-Tallis lane until it put it out, a week before its notice
(the ninth), with the ferry on the water. "Twenty-nine lights" counts lit
cities and never counted the Orm lane-lamp. Little Brand: nine years dark,
sixty-one people, accounts kept in knots on a net. Its lamp was opened
fully as they left and was seen. Ilse's eighth hearing took testimony from
all eleven; the record (Tobb made two copies) travels with her.

**Wyn and Nan.** Wyn married a cooper on Tessaly (nineteen years dark) and
moved to Little Brand eight years ago to keep its lamp; the cooper is dead.
Nan still floats Tessaly. Ren carries Wyn's letter to Nan, undelivered.

**The Line.** In Ren's ear since he was fifteen. On and unanswered leaving
the Reach; Thorne's last words on it were an order to leave the Tribune and
save himself; it died as the lane closed and did not reopen when they came
back onto the map. It opened once more, relayed from the Audit cells in
Tallis, for the order he declined, and closed.

**Thorne.** Was Halcyon Recoveries; collected Ren at thirty-nine and moved
to the Academy to raise him. Sent the Saltmere assay (Ren's photographs)
through the Tallis clearing house herself and gave her wrists to Sable. She
is in Audit custody at Tallis.

**Sable.** Holds a signed, undated warrant for Ren (the Audit file says Ren
touched the Reels) and has not served it. He knows Ren placed the shell
without knowing its purpose. He does not yet know the salt is running out:
he believes the assay shows one poor pit, and stopped Ren telling him more.

**Maravel.** Suspended on the shell recording ("It is lawful. I had hoped to
find that it was not."), then moved to a cell in the capital on a charge of
conspiracy in the Drawing, which Corvane can withdraw. Corvane offered his
release for a renewal on emergency terms; Ilse entered the offer in the
record and declined it. Her father has read that record and approves.

**Corvane's figures.** Salt for forty lights for six years, or twenty-nine
for twenty. He chose each of the eleven. Emergency terms: the Mint may close
a lane without a hearing and holds the Reels, for one term. At Tallis the
Mint hall's line broke and the Commons line at Ropewalk (ledger in the
street, places chalked on a wall) held; he saw only the first. The mark
closed at sixty-one. He has since put out one more light, not yet named.

**Law, by number.** Settlement section four: the Tribune shall hear the
citizens. Article six: testimony before the Tribune is privileged. Section
nine: the drum audit. Investiture clause two: no kin, no trade. Surety
contract clause four: the Tribune may not dismiss her Surety; clause nine

disparagement; clause twenty: the true account.

**Other numbers.** The scholarship dormitory has sixty beds. Ansel Pryce,

days from its hearing. The Circuit is seven cities: Saltmere, Halcyon
Reach and Tallis are heard; four remain, then the capital.

## Things decided here that chapters 1 to 5 left open

These are the answers. They are binding on all three writers.

**The Circuit.** Seven cities, in this order: Saltmere, Halcyon Reach,
Tallis, **Kedge** (chapter 6), **Pellam** and **Scarholm** (both heard in
passage, in the chapter 7 prologue), and **Vessery** last (chapter 7: Ilse
promised the docks a hearing, `ch1.s7.c05`). Little Brand was the eighth
hearing and is not one of the seven. The capital is **Cresset**; its lamp
is the **Founders' Lamp**, the oldest and brightest in Aurelle.

**Renewal Day.** The Casting Vote is cast in the Assembly at Cresset on a
fixed day. A new lot may be drawn only while a whole Circuit can still be
heard before that day (that is how a Tribune "drowned on the Circuit" was
replaced). After that, a vote not cast on the day is a lapse. Chapter 8
opens three days before Renewal Day.

**The one more light** (`ch5` epilogue: "It has its own page"). It was
**Saltmere**, capped on the twenty-third day of a thirty-day stay. It is
the first time the Mint has walked through an order of the Benches. Lit
cities are now **twenty-eight**; dark islands are **twelve**. Saltmere is
alive: inside a week Orm town taught it the ash by lamp.

**The dim flame.** Kelp-ash and stillsalt, three parts to one, burn a
grey-violet flame on a quarter of the salt (seventy-five casks a year, not
three hundred). On the Chancellor's own first figure that is **forty lights
for twenty-four years** ("forty for twenty-four"): better than his
twenty-nine for twenty, with nobody dark. It has three costs and writers
must keep all three. (1) A dim lamp lays calm about half as far, so a lane
holds only between neighbours: every island must burn, the twelve dark ones
included, because they are the stepping stones. Twenty-eight dim lights do
not make a map. Forty do. (2) A dim lane is **one hull wide**: no convoy,
no deep carrier, no troop ship. Trade shrinks. An army cannot be moved on
it. (3) It only works if every lamp-house opens its hopper to be counted by
its neighbours, because one island holding salt back gains years at
everyone's cost. It is Ropewalk's ledger in the street (`ch5.s4`), for
forty cities. It stinks.

**The rule of the jettison** (the dockers call it **average**). The oldest
law on any quay, and Halcyon's own founding trade as a cargo insurer: *what
is thrown over to save the ship is paid for by every hold that came home.*
A jettison is lawful only in common peril, declared openly by the master,
and adjusted afterward among all who were saved. A secret jettison is
theft. This is Ilse's "new terms": the Chancellor threw twelve islands over
to keep twenty-eight afloat, declared nothing, and adjusted nothing. Under
average no light is put out for arrears; a shortage is borne by every lamp
alike. The **Average Schedule** is this rule written as treaty terms. The
Chancellor's **Emergency Schedule** is its opposite: one office chooses who
goes over.

**Who rigged the Drawing.** Two frauds, one in the other's shadow. The Mint
struck arrears names from the drum, lawfully, and had the Assembly waive the
section nine audit so that nobody would count (Corvane's drafting: "people
in arrears vote their fear"). Halcyon used the waived audit to weight the
third reel: a lead plumb-weight behind the orb stop, set with the
reel-warden's key by **Thorne herself**, once an hour before Ren drew and
once before midnight. Three orbs open one drawer of the drum, and that
drawer held one slip. Ren never touched the Reels. The Audit's file naming
him was supplied, unsigned, by Halcyon, as a leash on its own asset, and
the Chancellor's office accepted it because a Halcyon cadet rigging the
Drawing is grounds for the Mint to hold the Reels.

**Why Ilse.** Halcyon priced three roads to a lapse and chose the name that
served all three. *Steer:* eighteen, and expected to be frightened. *Stop:*
one Surety, theirs. *Void:* her father certified the Drawing, so a
recording of him wishing the waiver unlawful (the shell, `ch1.s2`) could
suspend him and, at need, void her investiture: no Tribune, no vote, a
lapse. And a Hill girl would set the docks rioting. "We priced her wrong on
every line."

**The shot on the pier** (`ch1.s5`). **Sable** fired it, in a docker's coat
supplied to him, on a sealed instruction in the Chancellor's cipher: hit a
lantern, not the girl; bring the Tribune under Audit protection. He was the
best shot of his intake and he hit a lantern. Within the hour the bond went
to Halcyon instead, and he understood that the shot had been for the coat:
the docks were to be blamed. It is the first time Corvane manufactured the
crowd he was sure of. Chapter 8 is the second. Ren half knew on the pier
("he told himself it was because he was not certain") and it is the one
thing he never disclosed on Little Brand. Ilse's finding on Ren (`ch3.s7`:
"innocent of the purpose, and guilty of the act") is word for word the
finding on Sable. The brothers are level.

**The Schedule and the Chancellor.** Corvane did read Schedule One
(`ch1.s6.c23`). He let it run. He meant to produce the vote bond himself,
at Cresset, and take the Reels from the only army for sale. He believed he
alone could manage Halcyon.

**The nine numbered accounts.** Nine city treasuries, the nine brightest
and richest, **Scarholm** first among them (if a second and third name are
ever needed: **Eddring** and **High Vane**). Halcyon sold their councils
the secret of the shortage and then the remedy: regiments on retainer for
the day the treaty dies, with the vote bond as their guarantee. So the
people who broke when they were told the sum were not a crowd. They were
nine councils with a treasury each. The nine brokers are Halcyon's own and
are a different nine.

**The three earlier Tribunes.** Halcyon stood surety for all three. One was
paid (the one who cried at Saltmere and bought a house). One drowned in
honest weather. One ran rather than be bought: the Compact rowed him out,
entered as rope, and he is **the old man of Maund** with nine goats
(`ch4.s5.c22`: "the goats abstain"). One line, in chapter 6 or 7, no more.

**The bursar's letter.** Thorne drafted its last paragraph to turn on the
word *default* (`ch3.s5.c19-c21`). A bond whose object is to prevent a
treaty vote is void, so an instruction under it cannot be defaulted on, so
the write-off of Ren's thirteen years would stand. It needs a court to say
so. Whether a court ever does depends on the ending. It was her second
book, hidden inside the first.

**Carrow.** The keeper is his mother, **Tamsin Lowrie**. She alone said yes
to the clinic boat, because he had the fever, and for thirteen years she
has believed she gave her son to a company. The boat saved his life: the
same fever took his youngest sister, **Wenna**, aged two, the first dark
winter, with the lane shut and no clinic. His other sisters live: **Kerra**
(nine then, twenty-two now) and **Jenna** (four then, seventeen now). His
father was dead before the boat came; do not invent him further. Carrow
had three hundred and ten people when the order was signed.

**The Ledger.** The narrator is **Ilse, in old age**. For the term the
Tribune had no kin and no trade, so she wrote of the Tribune in the third
person, as the law required. The entries she did not witness are in a
second hand: his. This is told once, in the last lines of the chapter 8
epilogue, in both endings. Chapters 6 and 7 must not hint harder than
chapters 1 to 5 already do.

**No more rigged pulls.** `riggedSpin: null` in chapters 6, 7 and 8. The
only rigged pull in the game is the first. After chapter 7 the Reels have
been opened in public; a scripted line on them would be a lie the game
